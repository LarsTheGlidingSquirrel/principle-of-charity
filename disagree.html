Disagree Respectfully

~ healthy disagreement ~ constructive feedback ~ avoid fights
To disagree respectfully means

Try to really understand the other person's point and show that you understood

Allow different views to exist and face them with curiosity

Try to find common ground

Don't try to "win" or "persuade" - you are on the same team trying to learn from each other and find solutions

This helps to
Make conversations more constructive Reduce defensiveness Create space for better ideas

Listen

When somebody is talking or you read a message by them try to ...

Listen carefully and put your focus on understanding what they say

Your mind might be tempted to be busy with other tasks while listening

For example many of us stop listening and try to come up with an answer while the other is still talking

Or we might stop listening because we are already busy spinning up a story or interpretation

Next time you talk to someone try to notice moments where your mind stops listening and is occupied with other things.

Ask questions

Ask them to give more details

We are trying to figure out what the other wants to communicate. So getting information directly from them is the most accurate information we can get.

Asking questions is often the best approach to figure out what somebody wants to communicate Next time you talk to someone try to ask them to explain in more detail what they want to communicate and observe what it does to the conversation.

1. Help them feel understood

Before you disagree, show that you heard the main point of their message.

People are more likely to listen when they feel understood. It sounds like you are worried our food will be easy to steal if we do not improve protection.

Then add your disagreement from that shared understanding.

2. Make them feel respected

Respect does not mean agreeing. It means treating the person with care while you disagree.

Respect keeps the conversation constructive and prevents it from turning personal.
3. Don't treat each other like opponents

Disagreement is a shared search for a better answer, not a competition.

Focus on the problem, not on winning the other person over. We both want the same thing: enough food for winter. We just see different ways to get there.
4. Be curious about their perspective

Ask what worries them, what they want, and why they chose that idea.

Genuine curiosity helps you learn and makes the other person feel heard.
Disagreeing respectfully means understanding, respecting, cooperating, and staying curious.

If you follow these steps, your disagreement can still be honest while remaining constructive.

A respectful disagreement can help you arrive at a better idea together.
